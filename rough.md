# Dual-Head TLSTM — Mathematical Specification

**A co-adaptive, calibrated, selective forecasting engine**

Version 2.0 · Formal revision

---

## 0. Preface

### 0.1 Scope

This document specifies, at the level of measure-theoretic probability and
differentiable optimization, a two-headed recurrent forecasting system for
next-day directional prediction on a cross-section of financial assets. Every
structural claim is stated as a definition, assumption, lemma, proposition,
theorem, or remark, and every claim that is provable under the stated
assumptions is proved. Claims that are *not* provable (non-convex convergence,
finite-sample calibration) are stated as such and bounded where possible.

## 1. Probabilistic setting

### 1.1 The filtered probability space

Let $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})$ be a probability space carrying a
discrete-time filtration $\{\mathcal{F}_t\}_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}$,

$$
\mathcal{F}_s \subseteq \mathcal{F}_t \subseteq \mathcal{F}
\qquad \text{for all } s \le t .
$$

**Interpretation.** $\mathcal{F}_t$ is the $\sigma$-algebra generated by all
information observable at or before the close of trading day $t$: prices,
volumes, and macro series released up to and including that close.

### 1.2 Observables

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a finite universe of assets, $\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert = N$.
For $a\in\mathcal{A}$ and $t\in\mathbb{Z}$:

- $C_{a,t} > 0$ — closing price,
- $V_{a,t} \ge 0$ — traded volume.

**Assumption A1 (adaptedness of raw data).** $C_{a,t}$ and $V_{a,t}$ are
$\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable for every $a$ and every $t$.

### 1.3 The prediction target

**Definition 1.1 (direction label).**

$$
Y_{a,t+1} \;=\; \mathbf{1}\{C_{a,t+1} > C_{a,t}\} \;\in\; \{0,1\}.
$$

**Remark 1.2 (the fundamental constraint).** $Y_{a,t+1}$ is
$\mathcal{F}_{t+1}$-measurable and, except in degenerate cases, **not**
$\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable. The entire causality apparatus of §2 exists to
guarantee that no object used to produce a forecast of $Y_{a,t+1}$ is
measurable with respect to anything finer than $\mathcal{F}_t$.

### 1.4 Standing assumptions

| ID | Statement |
|---|---|
| **A1** | Raw price and volume series are $\mathcal{F}_t$-adapted (§1.2). |
| **A2** | All feature maps are Borel-measurable functions of finitely many lagged adapted quantities. |
| **A3** | Macro series are released with non-negative delay and are forward-filled only (§3.4). |
| **A4** | The data-set is split chronologically into three contiguous index blocks (§4.2). |
| **A5** | All fitted preprocessing statistics are estimated on the training block only. |
| **A6** | Losses are almost-surely differentiable in all parameters at the iterates visited by the optimizer. |

Assumptions **A1–A5** are the causality assumptions; **A6** is the optimization
assumption. Every result in §2–§5 depends only on **A1–A5**; results in §9 and
§14 additionally require **A6**.

## 2. Causality and the no-leakage principle

### 2.1 Definitions

**Definition 2.1 (causal feature map).** A family of maps
$\varphi_t:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^d$ is **causal**, or
$\{\mathcal{F}_t\}$-adapted, if $\varphi_t$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable for
every $t$.

**Definition 2.2 (non-anticipative predictor).** A predictor
$\widehat{Y}_{t+1}$ of $Y_{t+1}$ is **non-anticipative** if it is
$\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable.

**Definition 2.3 (look-ahead leakage).** A system exhibits **look-ahead
leakage** at time $t$ if some quantity it uses to form $\widehat{Y}_{t+1}$
fails to be $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable, i.e. depends non-trivially on
$\mathcal{F}_{t+k}\setminus\mathcal{F}_t$ for some $k>0$.

### 2.2 Closure of causality under composition

**Lemma 2.4 (composition preserves causality).** Let
$\varphi^{(1)},\dots,\varphi^{(n)}$ be $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable random
elements with values in $\mathbb{R}^{d_1},\dots,\mathbb{R}^{d_n}$, and let

$$
g : \mathbb{R}^{d_1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{d_n} \to \mathbb{R}^{m}
$$

be Borel-measurable. Then $g(\varphi^{(1)},\dots,\varphi^{(n)})$ is
$\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable.

*Proof.* The map $\omega \mapsto (\varphi^{(1)}(\omega),\dots,\varphi^{(n)}(\omega))$
is $\mathcal{F}_t/\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d_1+\cdots+d_n})$-measurable, since a
vector of measurable coordinates is measurable into the product $\sigma$-algebra,
which coincides with the Borel $\sigma$-algebra of the product for separable
metric spaces. Composition of a measurable map with a Borel map is measurable.
∎

**Corollary 2.5 (rolling statistics are causal).** For any $k\in\mathbb{N}$ and
any Borel $g:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}$, the statistic
$g(x_{t-k+1},\dots,x_t)$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable whenever $(x_s)$ is
adapted. In particular

$$
\sigma_{20,t} \;=\; \operatorname{sd}\big(r_{t-19},\dots,r_t\big),
\qquad
\mathrm{MA}_{k}(u)_t \;=\; \frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1} u_{t-j}
$$

are causal.

*Proof.* Immediate from Lemma 2.4 with $\varphi^{(i)} = x_{t-i+1}$, each of
which is $\mathcal{F}_{t-i+1}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable. ∎

**Remark 2.6 (what is excluded).** Centred rolling windows, two-sided filters
(e.g. Savitzky–Golay applied symmetrically), full-sample z-scores, and any
statistic indexed by $t+k$, $k>0$, are excluded by Corollary 2.5 because they
are not functions of $\{x_s\}_{s\le t}$ alone.

### 2.3 Missing-data handling

**Proposition 2.7 (forward-fill is causal; interpolation is not).** Let
$(M_\tau)_{\tau\in\mathcal{S}}$ be a series observed on a sparse index set
$\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}$, adapted in the sense that $M_\tau$ is
$\mathcal{F}_\tau$-measurable. Define

$$
\widetilde{M}_t \;=\; M_{\tau(t)},
\qquad
\tau(t) \;=\; \max\{\tau\in\mathcal{S} : \tau \le t\}.
$$

Then $(\widetilde{M}_t)$ is causal. By contrast, backward-fill
$\tau^{+}(t)=\min\{\tau\in\mathcal{S}:\tau\ge t\}$ and any linear interpolation
between a past and a future observation are **not** causal.

*Proof.* Fix $t$ and Borel $B$. Then

$$
\{\widetilde{M}_t \in B\}
= \bigcup_{\tau\in\mathcal{S},\,\tau\le t}
\Big( \{\tau(t)=\tau\} \cap \{M_\tau\in B\} \Big),
$$

a countable union of sets in $\mathcal{F}_t$, since $\{\tau(t)=\tau\}$ is
determined by the deterministic index set $\mathcal{S}$ and
$\{M_\tau\in B\}\in\mathcal{F}_\tau\subseteq\mathcal{F}_t$. For backward-fill,
$\widetilde{M}_t = M_{\tau^{+}(t)}$ with $\tau^{+}(t)>t$ on a set of positive
probability, and $M_{\tau^{+}(t)}$ is in general not
$\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable; the same holds for any convex combination assigning
non-zero weight to a future observation. ∎

### 2.4 Chronological splitting as $\sigma$-algebra separation

**Definition 2.8 (chronological split).** Fix
$0 < T_1 < T_2 < T_3$ and set

$$
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{tr}} = \{1,\dots,T_1\},
\qquad
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{va}} = \{T_1+1,\dots,T_2\},
\qquad
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{te}} = \{T_2+1,\dots,T_3\}.
$$

**Definition 2.9 (embargo).** For window length $T$ and horizon $1$, the
**embargoed** split removes the $T$ indices immediately preceding each boundary
from the earlier block:

$$
\mathcal{T}^{\,\mathrm{emb}}_{\mathrm{tr}} = \{1,\dots,T_1-T\},
\qquad
\mathcal{T}^{\,\mathrm{emb}}_{\mathrm{va}} = \{T_1+1,\dots,T_2-T\}.
$$

**Theorem 2.10 (no-leakage under chronological splitting).** Assume A1–A5 and
the embargo of Definition 2.9. Let $\theta$ denote any parameter estimated from
$\mathcal{T}^{\,\mathrm{emb}}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ and $g$ any calibration map
estimated from $\mathcal{T}^{\,\mathrm{emb}}_{\mathrm{va}}$. Then

$$
\theta \ \text{is } \mathcal{F}_{T_1}\text{-measurable},
\qquad
g \ \text{is } \mathcal{F}_{T_2}\text{-measurable},
$$

and for every $t\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{te}}$ the reported forecast
$\widehat{Y}_{a,t+1}$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable. Consequently no statistic
of the test block influences any fitted object.

*Proof.* Estimation of $\theta$ is a Borel function of the finite collection
$\{(X_{a,s}, Y_{a,s+1}) : s \le T_1 - T\}$, every element of which is
$\mathcal{F}_{T_1}$-measurable; Lemma 2.4 gives $\mathcal{F}_{T_1}$-measurability
of $\theta$. The same argument applied to the validation block, whose targets
are $\mathcal{F}_{T_2}$-measurable, gives the claim for $g$. At test time the
forecast is $g\big(f(\psi_\theta(X_{a,t}))\big)$ with $X_{a,t}$
$\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable by Corollary 2.5 and $\theta,g$ measurable with
respect to coarser $\sigma$-algebras $\mathcal{F}_{T_1},\mathcal{F}_{T_2}
\subseteq\mathcal{F}_t$ for $t > T_2$. Lemma 2.4 applies once more. ∎

**Remark 2.11 (why the embargo matters).** Without the embargo, a window
$X_{a,t}$ with $t \le T_1$ but $t+1 > T_1$ contributes a training gradient that
depends on $Y_{a,T_1+1}$, the first validation label. The leak is small in
volume — $O(1)$ samples per boundary per asset — but it is a genuine violation
of Theorem 2.10, and the theorem is stated only for the embargoed split for
that reason.

### 2.5 Per-asset windowing

**Proposition 2.12 (windows must not cross asset boundaries).** Let the panel
be stored as a vertical concatenation of per-asset blocks and let windows be
cut over the concatenated row index. Then windows straddling a block boundary
are invalid, for either of two reasons:

1. **Label misalignment.** The window contains rows of asset $a$ and asset $a'$
   while the aligned target is $Y_{a',\,t'+1}$ for a single $a'$; the sample no
   longer represents the conditional law
   $\mathcal{L}(Y_{a',t'+1}\mid \mathcal{F}^{a'}_{t'})$ that the model is
   claimed to estimate.
2. **Leakage.** If the blocks are ordered so that asset $a$'s later dates
   precede asset $a'$'s earlier dates, a straddling window used to predict
   $Y_{a',t'+1}$ contains observations of asset $a$ at calendar times
   $s > t'+1$, which are not $\mathcal{F}_{t'}$-measurable.

*Proof.* (1) is a statement about the estimand, not measurability: the
empirical risk minimized is that of a different conditional expectation.
(2) follows from Definition 2.3 applied to the calendar time-stamps of the
included rows. ∎

**Specification 2.13.** Windows are constructed per asset:

$$
X_{a,t} \;=\; \big[\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{a,t-T+1},\,\dots,\,\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{a,t}\big]^{\top}
\;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{T\times d},
$$

and only then pooled into batches.

## 3. Feature construction

Fix an asset $a$; the index is suppressed where unambiguous.

### 3.1 Price-derived features

**Definition 3.1 (log return over $k$ days).**

$$
r^{(k)}_t \;=\; \log C_t - \log C_{t-k}, \qquad k \in \{1,3\}.
$$

**Definition 3.2 (realized volatility).**

$$
\sigma_{20,t}
\;=\;
\sqrt{\frac{1}{19}\sum_{j=0}^{19}\big(r^{(1)}_{t-j} - \bar r_t\big)^2},
\qquad
\bar r_t = \frac{1}{20}\sum_{j=0}^{19} r^{(1)}_{t-j}.
$$

**Definition 3.3 (moving-average ratio).**

$$
\mathrm{MA5Ratio}_t \;=\; \frac{C_t}{\frac{1}{5}\sum_{j=0}^{4} C_{t-j}} \;-\; 1 .
$$

**Definition 3.4 (Wilder relative strength index, period $n=14$).** With

$$
U_t = \max(C_t - C_{t-1},\,0),
\qquad
D_t = \max(C_{t-1} - C_t,\,0),
$$

define the Wilder-smoothed averages recursively by

$$
\bar U_t = \Big(1-\tfrac{1}{n}\Big)\bar U_{t-1} + \tfrac{1}{n}U_t,
\qquad
\bar D_t = \Big(1-\tfrac{1}{n}\Big)\bar D_{t-1} + \tfrac{1}{n}D_t,
$$

and set

$$
\mathrm{RSI}_t \;=\; 100\left(1 - \frac{1}{1 + \bar U_t / (\bar D_t + \epsilon)}\right)
\;\in\;[0,100].
$$

**Definition 3.5 (relative volume).**

$$
\Delta V^{\%}_t \;=\; \frac{V_t}{\mathrm{MA}_{20}(V)_t + \epsilon} \;-\; 1 .
$$

### 3.2 Macro features

Let $\mathrm{VIX}_t$, $\mathrm{DXY}_t$, $\mathrm{TNX}_t$ denote the
forward-filled (Proposition 2.7) levels of the volatility index, dollar index,
and ten-year yield proxy, and define for $Z \in \{\mathrm{VIX},\mathrm{DXY},\mathrm{TNX}\}$

$$
\Delta Z^{\%}_t \;=\; \frac{Z_t}{Z_{t-1}} \;-\; 1 .
$$

### 3.3 The feature vector

$$
\mathbf{x}_t =
\begin{bmatrix}
r^{(1)}_t & r^{(3)}_t & \sigma_{20,t} & \mathrm{MA5Ratio}_t & \mathrm{RSI}_t & \Delta V^{\%}_t &
\mathrm{VIX}_t & \mathrm{DXY}_t & \mathrm{TNX}_t & \Delta\mathrm{VIX}^{\%}_t & \Delta\mathrm{DXY}^{\%}_t & \Delta\mathrm{TNX}^{\%}_t
\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
\in \mathbb{R}^{12}.
$$

**Proposition 3.6 (the feature map is causal).** Under A1–A3,
$\mathbf{x}_t$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable, and $d = 12$.

*Proof.* Definitions 3.1–3.5 express each coordinate as a Borel function of
$\{C_s, V_s\}_{s\le t}$; the recursions in Definition 3.4 are, by induction,
Borel functions of $\{C_s\}_{s\le t}$ given an $\mathcal{F}_{t_0}$-measurable
initialization. Macro coordinates are causal by Proposition 2.7. Lemma 2.4
closes the argument coordinatewise. ∎

### 3.4 Release-delay caveat

**Remark 3.7.** Proposition 2.7 guarantees causality relative to the
*observation* index $\tau$. If a macro series carries a publication lag
$\delta \ge 1$ days, A3 requires that $\widetilde M_t$ be built from
$\tau(t) = \max\{\tau \in \mathcal{S} : \tau + \delta \le t\}$. Using the
reference date rather than the release date is a leakage channel that the
forward-fill lemma does **not** rule out.

## 4. Preprocessing

### 4.1 Standardization

**Definition 4.1 (train-fold affine standardization).** For coordinate
$j\in\{1,\dots,d\}$, using the pooled training index set
$\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{tr}} = \{(a,t): a\in\mathcal{A},\ t\in\mathcal{T}^{\,\mathrm{emb}}_{\mathrm{tr}}\}$,

$$
\mu_j = \frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{tr}}\rvert}\sum_{(a,t)\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{tr}}} x_{a,t,j},
\qquad
s_j^2 = \frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{tr}}\rvert-1}\sum_{(a,t)\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{tr}}} (x_{a,t,j}-\mu_j)^2,
$$

$$
\widetilde x_{a,t,j} = \frac{x_{a,t,j} - \mu_j}{s_j + \epsilon},
\qquad \epsilon > 0 \text{ fixed}.
$$

**Lemma 4.2 (standardization preserves causality).** The pair $(\mu_j,s_j)$ is
$\mathcal{F}_{T_1}$-measurable, and for every $t$ the standardized feature
$\widetilde x_{a,t,j}$ is $\mathcal{F}_{\max(t,T_1)}$-measurable; in particular
it is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable for all $t \ge T_1$, which covers the
validation and test blocks.

*Proof.* $(\mu_j,s_j)$ is a Borel function of training-block features, hence
$\mathcal{F}_{T_1}$-measurable by Lemma 2.4 and Proposition 3.6. The map
$x \mapsto (x-\mu_j)/(s_j+\epsilon)$ is Borel jointly in
$(x,\mu_j,s_j)$, and $s_j + \epsilon > 0$ almost surely, so no measurability is
lost. Apply Lemma 2.4. ∎

**Remark 4.3 (two failure modes).** (i) Computing $(\mu_j,s_j)$ over the full
sample violates A5 and Theorem 2.10. (ii) Computing them per asset over that
asset's full history is the same violation, disguised. Per-asset statistics are
admissible only if estimated on $\mathcal{T}^{\,\mathrm{emb}}_{\mathrm{tr}}$
alone, or computed as an expanding-window statistic, which is causal by
Corollary 2.5.

## 5. Windowing and batch algebra

**Definition 5.1.** With window length $T$ and $d=12$, the input tensor for
asset $a$ at time $t$ is $X_{a,t}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times d}$ as in
Specification 2.13, with aligned target $Y_{a,t+1}$.

**Definition 5.2 (minibatch).** A minibatch is a finite set
$\mathcal{B}\subset \mathcal{A}\times\mathbb{Z}$ of index pairs with
$\lvert\mathcal{B}\rvert = B$, assembled into

$$
\mathcal{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{B\times T\times d},
\qquad
\mathbf{y} \in \{0,1\}^{B}.
$$

**Constraint 5.3.** No operation in the network mixes the batch axis. Formally,
the network $F$ satisfies

$$
F(\mathcal{X})_b = F_1(\mathcal{X}_b)
\qquad\text{for every } b\in\{1,\dots,B\},
$$

for a single-sample map $F_1$. This excludes batch normalization, batch-level
whitening, and any cross-sample attention, all of which would make the
prediction for sample $b$ depend on other samples — which at inference time may
be drawn from other assets or other dates.

## 6. Shared encoder

### 6.1 Recurrent cell

**Definition 6.1 (LSTM cell).** Let $h\in\mathbb{N}$ be the hidden width. With
$W_\bullet \in\mathbb{R}^{h\times d}$, $U_\bullet\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times h}$,
$b_\bullet\in\mathbb{R}^{h}$ for $\bullet\in\{f,i,o,c\}$, and
$(h_0,c_0) = (\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})$, define for $i=1,\dots,T$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_i &= \mathrm{sigm}\big(W_f x_i + U_f h_{i-1} + b_f\big), \\
\iota_i &= \mathrm{sigm}\big(W_i x_i + U_i h_{i-1} + b_i\big), \\
o_i &= \mathrm{sigm}\big(W_o x_i + U_o h_{i-1} + b_o\big), \\
\tilde c_i &= \tanh\big(W_c x_i + U_c h_{i-1} + b_c\big), \\
c_i &= f_i \odot c_{i-1} + \iota_i \odot \tilde c_i, \\
h_i &= o_i \odot \tanh(c_i).
\end{aligned}
$$

Write $\theta$ for the collection of all encoder parameters.

**Definition 6.2 (representation).** The shared representation is either the
terminal state or the temporal mean,

$$
\psi_\theta(X_{a,t}) \;=\; h_T
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\psi_\theta(X_{a,t}) \;=\; \bar h \;=\; \frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T} h_i,
\qquad \psi_\theta(X_{a,t})\in\mathbb{R}^{h}.
$$

The choice is fixed before training and is not a tuned quantity at test time.

### 6.2 Non-anticipativity

**Lemma 6.3.** $\psi_\theta(X_{a,t})$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable for every
$t \ge T_1$.

*Proof.* The recursion of Definition 6.1 is a finite composition of affine maps
and the continuous functions $\mathrm{sigm}$, $\tanh$, and coordinatewise
product; it is therefore Borel in $(X_{a,t},\theta)$. $X_{a,t}$ is
$\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable by Lemma 4.2 and Specification 2.13; $\theta$ is
$\mathcal{F}_{T_1}$-measurable by Theorem 2.10. Lemma 2.4 gives the result. ∎

**Remark 6.4 (temporal causality inside the window).** $h_i$ depends only on
$x_1,\dots,x_i$. A bidirectional encoder would make $h_i$ depend on
$x_{i+1},\dots,x_T$. Within a completed window this is *not* leakage relative
to $\mathcal{F}_t$, since all of $X_{a,t}$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable; but it
destroys the interpretation of $h_i$ as a filtered state and is excluded by
Definition 6.1 for that reason, not for a measurability reason.

## 7. Head A — primary prediction

Let $f_A(\,\cdot\,;\theta_A):\mathbb{R}^{h}\to\mathbb{R}$ be affine or a
multilayer perceptron.

**Definition 7.1 (regression form).**

$$
\widehat Y^{A}_{a,t+1} \;=\; f_A\big(\psi_\theta(X_{a,t});\theta_A\big).
$$

**Definition 7.2 (classification form).**

$$
p^{A}_{a,t+1} = \mathrm{sigm}\Big(f_A\big(\psi_\theta(X_{a,t});\theta_A\big)\Big),
\qquad
\widehat Y^{A}_{a,t+1} = \mathbf{1}\{p^{A}_{a,t+1} \ge \tfrac12\}.
$$

**Corollary 7.3.** Both forms are non-anticipative.

*Proof.* Lemma 6.3 plus Lemma 2.4; thresholding at $\tfrac12$ is Borel. ∎

## 8. Head B — auxiliary prediction

Head B shares $\psi_\theta$ and instantiates exactly one of three roles, fixed
before training. Let $f_B(\,\cdot\,;\theta_B):\mathbb{R}^h\to\mathbb{R}$.

### 8.1 Role I — error correction

**Definition 8.1.**

$$
e_{a,t+1} \;=\; \widehat Y^{A}_{a,t+1} - Y_{a,t+1},
\qquad
\widehat e_{a,t+1} \;=\; f_B\big(\psi_\theta(X_{a,t});\theta_B\big),
$$

$$
\widehat Y^{\mathrm{final}}_{a,t+1} \;=\; \widehat Y^{A}_{a,t+1} - \widehat e_{a,t+1}.
$$

### 8.2 Role II — conditional volatility

**Definition 8.2.**

$$
\widehat\sigma_{a,t+1} \;=\; \mathrm{softplus}\Big(f_B\big(\psi_\theta(X_{a,t});\theta_B\big)\Big) + \epsilon
\;>\; 0 .
$$

Softplus is preferred to $\exp$: it is $1$-Lipschitz, so the gradient of the
Gaussian negative log-likelihood in $\widehat\sigma$ cannot blow up under large
pre-activations.

### 8.3 Role III — confidence / selective prediction

**Definition 8.3.**

$$
q_{a,t+1} \;=\; \mathrm{sigm}\Big(f_B\big(\psi_\theta(X_{a,t});\theta_B\big)\Big)
\;\approx\;
\mathbb{P}\Big(Y_{a,t+1} = \widehat Y^{A}_{a,t+1} \;\Big|\; \mathcal{F}_t\Big).
$$

**Lemma 8.4.** Each of Definitions 8.1–8.3 yields an
$\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable output.

*Proof.* Identical to Corollary 7.3. ∎

## 9. Composite objective

### 9.1 Error-correction instantiation

$$
\mathcal{L}_A(\theta,\theta_A)
= \frac{1}{B}\sum_{(a,t)\in\mathcal{B}}
\big(Y_{a,t+1} - \widehat Y^{A}_{a,t+1}\big)^2 ,
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}_B(\theta,\theta_A,\theta_B)
= \frac{1}{B}\sum_{(a,t)\in\mathcal{B}}
\big(e_{a,t+1} - \widehat e_{a,t+1}\big)^2 .
$$

**Lemma 9.1 (residual identity).**

$$
\big(e_{a,t+1} - \widehat e_{a,t+1}\big)^2
= \big(Y_{a,t+1} - \widehat Y^{\mathrm{final}}_{a,t+1}\big)^2 .
$$

*Proof.* $Y - \widehat Y^{\mathrm{final}}
= Y - \widehat Y^{A} + \widehat e
= -\big(e - \widehat e\big)$; square both sides. ∎

### 9.2 Classification instantiation

$$
\mathcal{L}_A = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{(a,t)\in\mathcal{B}}
\mathrm{BCE}\big(Y_{a,t+1},\, p^{A}_{a,t+1}\big),
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}_B = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{(a,t)\in\mathcal{B}}
\mathrm{BCE}\Big(\mathrm{sg}\big[\mathbf{1}\{Y_{a,t+1} = \widehat Y^{A}_{a,t+1}\}\big],\; q_{a,t+1}\Big),
$$

with $\mathrm{BCE}(y,p) = -y\log p - (1-y)\log(1-p)$.

**Remark 9.2 (why the stop-gradient is mandatory).** The Head-B target
$\mathbf{1}\{Y = \widehat Y^{A}\}$ is a thresholded function of $p^{A}$ and is
therefore piecewise constant: its derivative with respect to $(\theta,\theta_A)$
is zero almost everywhere and undefined on the decision boundary. Writing
$\mathrm{sg}[\cdot]$ makes this explicit rather than leaving it to
autodifferentiation to discover, and it is what licenses the game-theoretic
reading in Proposition 10.5.

### 9.3 Total objective

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}(\theta,\theta_A,\theta_B)
= \mathcal{L}_A + \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_B,
\qquad \lambda > 0 .
$$

**Proposition 9.3 (identifiability of the decomposition).** In the
error-correction instantiation with gradients allowed to flow through
$e_{a,t+1}$, the pair
$\big(\widehat Y^{A},\widehat e\big)$ is not identified by $\mathcal{L}_B$
alone: for any $c\in\mathbb{R}$ the substitution
$\widehat Y^{A}\mapsto \widehat Y^{A}+c$, $\widehat e\mapsto\widehat e + c$
leaves $\mathcal{L}_B$ invariant. Identification is supplied by
$\mathcal{L}_A$, not by the positivity of $\lambda$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 9.1, $\mathcal{L}_B$ depends on the pair only through the
difference $\widehat Y^{A} - \widehat e = \widehat Y^{\mathrm{final}}$, which is
invariant under the stated shift. $\mathcal{L}_A$ depends on
$\widehat Y^{A}$ directly and is strictly convex in it, so
$\mathcal{L}_A + \lambda\mathcal{L}_B$ has a unique minimizer in
$\widehat Y^{A}$ for fixed $\widehat Y^{\mathrm{final}}$. ∎

**Remark 9.4 (corrected claim).** The earlier draft of this specification
attributed the prevention of degenerate solutions to $\lambda$. Proposition 9.3
shows the correct attribution: $\lambda$ trades off the two objectives, while
$\mathcal{L}_A$ is what pins down the decomposition. A large $\lambda$ does not
create degeneracy; a *missing* $\mathcal{L}_A$ does.

## 10. Gradient flow and co-adaptation

### 10.1 Decomposition of the encoder gradient

**Proposition 10.1.** Under A6, with $\psi = \psi_\theta(X)$,

$$
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}}{\partial \theta}
=
\underbrace{
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_A}{\partial \psi}\,
\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial \theta}
}_{\text{primary path}}
\;+\;
\lambda\,
\underbrace{
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_B}{\partial \psi}\,
\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial \theta}
}_{\text{auxiliary path}} .
$$

*Proof.* Chain rule applied to $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}
= \mathcal{L}_A(\psi_\theta(X);\theta_A) + \lambda\mathcal{L}_B(\psi_\theta(X);\theta_A,\theta_B)$,
using differentiability at the iterate (A6) and linearity of differentiation. ∎

**Theorem 10.2 (the shared representation is a compromise).** Let
$\theta^{\star}$ be a stationary point of $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}$ in
$\theta$. Then

$$
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_A}{\partial\theta}\Big|_{\theta^{\star}}
= -\,\lambda\,\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_B}{\partial\theta}\Big|_{\theta^{\star}} .
$$

Consequently $\theta^{\star}$ is a stationary point of $\mathcal{L}_A$ alone if
and only if $\partial\mathcal{L}_B/\partial\theta$ vanishes there. Whenever the
auxiliary gradient is non-zero, the learned encoder differs from the
single-task optimum, and the difference is first-order in $\lambda$.

*Proof.* Stationarity gives
$\partial\mathcal{L}_A/\partial\theta + \lambda\,\partial\mathcal{L}_B/\partial\theta = 0$,
which is the displayed identity; the equivalence and the sign statement follow
immediately since $\lambda>0$. ∎

**Remark 10.3.** Theorem 10.2 is the precise content of "co-adaptation". It is
weaker than a claim that the auxiliary task *improves* Head A: it says only
that the representation is displaced. Whether the displacement helps is an
empirical question about $\mathcal{L}_A$ evaluated out of sample, and is
answered in §16, not here.

### 10.2 Why the auxiliary loss shapes the representation

**Theorem 10.4 (error-predictability incentive).** Fix the encoder
$\psi=\psi_\theta$ and suppose Head B ranges over all square-integrable Borel
functions of $\psi$. Then

$$
\inf_{g \text{ Borel}} \ \mathbb{E}\Big[\big(e - g(\psi)\big)^2\Big]
\;=\;
\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathrm{Var}\big(e \mid \psi\big)\Big],
$$

attained at $g^{\star}(\psi) = \mathbb{E}[e\mid\psi]$. Consequently, for two
encoders $\psi,\psi'$, the attainable auxiliary risk strictly decreases when
moving from $\psi$ to $\psi'$ if and only if

$$
\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Var}(e\mid\psi')\big] \;<\; \mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Var}(e\mid\psi)\big].
$$

*Proof.* For any square-integrable $g$, by the tower property and
$\mathbb{E}\big[(e-\mathbb{E}[e\mid\psi])\,(\mathbb{E}[e\mid\psi]-g(\psi))\big]=0$,

$$
\mathbb{E}\big[(e-g(\psi))^2\big]
= \mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Var}(e\mid\psi)\big]
+ \mathbb{E}\big[(\mathbb{E}[e\mid\psi]-g(\psi))^2\big].
$$

The first term does not depend on $g$; the second is non-negative and vanishes
at $g^\star$. The "if and only if" is then a comparison of two infima. ∎

**Remark 10.4a (finite capacity).** Theorem 10.4 is an *attainability*
statement over all Borel $g$. With $f_B$ restricted to an affine or MLP family
$\mathcal{G}$, the infimum is replaced by
$\inf_{g\in\mathcal{G}}$ and the identity becomes the inequality
$\inf_{\mathcal{G}} \ge \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Var}(e\mid\psi)]$, with the gap equal
to the approximation error of $\mathcal{G}$ for $\mathbb{E}[e\mid\psi]$.

### 10.3 Game-theoretic reading

**Proposition 10.5 (exact potential game).** Consider three players with
parameter blocks $\theta$ (encoder), $\theta_A$, $\theta_B$ and individual
costs

$$
J_{\theta} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}},
\qquad
J_{\theta_A} = \mathcal{L}_A,
\qquad
J_{\theta_B} = \mathcal{L}_B .
$$

Assume the stop-gradient of Remark 9.2, so that $\mathcal{L}_B$ does not depend
on $\theta_A$. Then $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}$ is an exact potential for
this game up to positive rescaling:

$$
\frac{\partial J_{\theta}}{\partial\theta} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}}{\partial\theta},
\qquad
\frac{\partial J_{\theta_A}}{\partial\theta_A} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}}{\partial\theta_A},
\qquad
\lambda\,\frac{\partial J_{\theta_B}}{\partial\theta_B} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}}{\partial\theta_B}.
$$

Hence the stationary points of $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}$ coincide with the
first-order Nash equilibria of the game.

*Proof.* $\mathcal{L}_B$ is independent of $\theta_A$ by hypothesis, so
$\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}/\partial\theta_A
= \partial\mathcal{L}_A/\partial\theta_A$. $\mathcal{L}_A$ is independent of
$\theta_B$, so $\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}/\partial\theta_B
= \lambda\,\partial\mathcal{L}_B/\partial\theta_B$, and $\lambda>0$ preserves
zero sets. The encoder identity is definitional. A joint stationary point
therefore annihilates every player's own-gradient, and conversely. ∎

**Remark 10.6 (corrected claim).** The earlier draft described the two heads as
reaching a "Nash-like equilibrium" without argument. Proposition 10.5 supplies
the argument and its precondition: the equilibrium reading is valid **only**
under the stop-gradient. Without it, $\mathcal{L}_B$ depends on $\theta_A$, no
exact potential exists in general, and joint gradient descent is simply descent
on a single scalar objective — a multi-task optimization, not a game.

## 11. Information-theoretic view

**Proposition 11.1 (data-processing inequality).** Let $\psi = \psi_\theta(X)$
be a deterministic measurable function of $X$. Then for the error variable $e$,

$$
I(e;\psi) \;\le\; I(e;X),
$$

with equality if and only if $\psi$ is a sufficient statistic for $e$ in
$X$, i.e. $e \perp X \mid \psi$.

*Proof.* $e \to X \to \psi$ is a Markov chain because $\psi = \psi_\theta(X)$
carries no randomness beyond $X$. Apply the data-processing inequality; the
equality condition is the standard sufficiency condition. ∎

**Proposition 11.2 ($L^2$ surrogate).** Define the explained variance

$$
\mathcal{E}(\psi) \;=\; \mathrm{Var}(e) \;-\; \mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Var}(e\mid\psi)\big].
$$

Then minimizing the attainable auxiliary risk of Theorem 10.4 is equivalent to
maximizing $\mathcal{E}(\psi)$. If moreover $(e,\psi)$ is jointly Gaussian with
correlation $\rho$, then

$$
I(e;\psi) \;=\; -\tfrac12\log\big(1-\rho^2\big),
\qquad
\mathcal{E}(\psi) = \rho^2\,\mathrm{Var}(e),
$$

so $I$ is a strictly increasing function of $\mathcal{E}$.

*Proof.* The first claim is immediate from the law of total variance,
$\mathrm{Var}(e) = \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Var}(e\mid\psi)] + \mathrm{Var}(\mathbb{E}[e\mid\psi])$,
since $\mathrm{Var}(e)$ does not depend on $\psi$. The Gaussian identities are
standard. ∎

**Remark 11.3 (honest scope).** Outside the Gaussian case, $\mathcal{E}$ is a
second-order surrogate and is *not* a monotone transform of $I(e;\psi)$ in
general. Training on $\mathcal{L}_B$ therefore maximizes a
variance-explained criterion, and the mutual-information language of the
earlier draft should be read as an interpretation with a proof only under
Proposition 11.2's Gaussian hypothesis.

## 12. Isotonic calibration

### 12.1 Setup

Let $s_1,\dots,s_n\in\mathbb{R}$ be meta-learner scores on the validation block
and $y_1,\dots,y_n\in\{0,1\}$ the aligned labels.

**Definition 12.1 (isotonic least squares).**

$$
g^{\star} \in \arg\min_{g \ \text{non-decreasing}} \ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(g(s_i) - y_i\big)^2 .
$$

### 12.2 Optimality of PAV

**Theorem 12.2 (pool-adjacent-violators solves Definition 12.1 globally).**
Sort the distinct scores $s_{(1)} < \cdots < s_{(m)}$, let $n_j$ be the
multiplicity of $s_{(j)}$ and $\bar y_j$ the mean label in that tie group. Then
Definition 12.1 is equivalent to the convex program

$$
\min_{u_1 \le \cdots \le u_m} \ \sum_{j=1}^{m} n_j\big(u_j - \bar y_j\big)^2 ,
$$

and the PAV algorithm returns its unique global minimizer, with each pooled
block assigned the label mean of that block.

*Proof.* **Reduction.** For fixed values $u_j$ on the $j$-th tie group,
$\sum_{i \in \text{group } j}(u_j-y_i)^2 = n_j(u_j-\bar y_j)^2 + \text{const}$,
which gives the displayed program; monotone $g$ is determined on the data by
$(u_j)$.

**Convexity.** The objective is a positive-definite quadratic and the
constraint set $\{u_1\le\cdots\le u_m\}$ is a closed convex polyhedron, so a
unique minimizer exists and KKT is necessary and sufficient.

**KKT.** With multipliers $\mu_j \ge 0$ for $u_j - u_{j+1}\le 0$, stationarity
reads

$$
2 n_j (u_j - \bar y_j) + \mu_j - \mu_{j-1} = 0,
\qquad
\mu_j\,(u_{j+1}-u_j) = 0,
\qquad \mu_0 = \mu_m = 0 .
$$

Summing stationarity over any maximal block $[p,q]$ on which the constraints
are active ($u_p=\cdots=u_q=:u$) telescopes the multipliers to
$\mu_{p-1}=\mu_q=0$ and yields
$u = \sum_{j=p}^{q} n_j\bar y_j \big/ \sum_{j=p}^{q} n_j$, the pooled mean.
On inactive constraints $\mu_j = 0$ forces $u_j = \bar y_j$.

**PAV.** The algorithm returns exactly a partition into maximal blocks, each
assigned its pooled mean, merging precisely when adjacent block means violate
monotonicity; the returned solution satisfies primal feasibility,
complementary slackness by construction, and dual feasibility
$\mu_j \ge 0$ by the merge rule. By convexity it is the global minimizer. ∎

### 12.3 Effect on ranking

**Proposition 12.3 (AUC under isotonic mapping).** If $g^{\star}$ is strictly
increasing on the observed support, the empirical AUC is unchanged. If
$g^{\star}$ is merely non-decreasing, let

$$
\pi \;=\; \frac{\#\{(i,k) : y_i=1,\ y_k=0,\ g^{\star}(s_i)=g^{\star}(s_k),\ s_i\ne s_k\}}{\#\{(i,k): y_i = 1,\ y_k = 0\}}
$$

be the fraction of positive–negative pairs newly tied by pooling. Then

$$
\big\lvert \mathrm{AUC}(g^{\star}\circ s) - \mathrm{AUC}(s) \big\rvert \;\le\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\pi .
$$

*Proof.* Empirical AUC is the average over positive–negative pairs of
$\mathbf{1}\{s_i>s_k\} + \tfrac12\mathbf{1}\{s_i=s_k\}$. A strictly increasing
map preserves every pairwise comparison, hence every summand. Under pooling,
only newly tied pairs change their contribution, from $1$ to $\tfrac12$ or from
$0$ to $\tfrac12$; each such change has magnitude $\tfrac12$ and there are a
$\pi$-fraction of them. The triangle inequality gives the bound. ∎

### 12.4 What calibration optimizes

**Proposition 12.4 (risk decomposition).** Let $p(s)=\mathbb{E}[y\mid s]$. For
any square-integrable $g$,

$$
\mathbb{E}\big[(y-g(s))^2\big]
=
\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\big[(y-p(s))^2\big]}_{\text{irreducible given } s}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\big[(p(s)-g(s))^2\big]}_{\text{calibration error}} .
$$

Isotonic regression minimizes the second term over non-decreasing $g$; it
cannot reduce the first.

*Proof.* Expand and use $\mathbb{E}\big[(y-p(s))(p(s)-g(s))\big] = 0$ by the
tower property. ∎

**Remark 12.5 (fitting block).** By A5 and Theorem 2.10, $g^{\star}$ is fitted
on $\mathcal{T}^{\,\mathrm{emb}}_{\mathrm{va}}$ and applied unchanged on
$\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{te}}$. Refitting on test scores would void Theorem 2.10
and every guarantee in §13.

## 13. Selective prediction

### 13.1 Decision rule

**Definition 13.1.** Given a calibrated probability
$p_{\mathrm{cal}} = g^{\star}(s)$ and a threshold $\tau\in[\tfrac12,1)$,

$$
\widehat Y =
\begin{cases}
1, & p_{\mathrm{cal}} \ge \tau, \\
0, & p_{\mathrm{cal}} \le 1-\tau, \\
\bot, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

**Definition 13.2 (coverage and selective accuracy).** With
$A = \{\widehat Y \ne \bot\}$ and $\kappa = \mathbb{P}(A) > 0$,

$$
\kappa = \mathbb{P}\big(\widehat Y \ne \bot\big),
\qquad
\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{sel}} = \mathbb{P}\big(\widehat Y = Y \,\big|\, A\big).
$$

### 13.2 Accuracy floor

**Theorem 13.3 (accuracy floor under perfect calibration).** Suppose
$p_{\mathrm{cal}}(X) = \mathbb{P}(Y=1\mid X)$ almost surely and
$\tau\ge\tfrac12$, $\kappa>0$. Then

$$
\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{sel}} \;=\; \mathbb{E}\Big[\max\big(p_{\mathrm{cal}},\,1-p_{\mathrm{cal}}\big)\,\Big|\,A\Big]
\;\ge\; \tau .
$$

*Proof.* $A$ is $\sigma(X)$-measurable, so conditioning on $X$ and applying the
tower property,

$$
\mathbb{P}(\widehat Y = Y,\,A)
= \mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbf{1}_A\,\mathbb{P}\big(Y=\widehat Y \mid X\big)\Big].
$$

On $\{p_{\mathrm{cal}}\ge\tau\}$ the action is $\widehat Y = 1$ and
$\mathbb{P}(Y=1\mid X) = p_{\mathrm{cal}} \ge \tau$; on
$\{p_{\mathrm{cal}}\le 1-\tau\}$ the action is $\widehat Y = 0$ and
$\mathbb{P}(Y=0\mid X) = 1-p_{\mathrm{cal}} \ge \tau$. In both cases the
conditional correctness probability equals
$\max(p_{\mathrm{cal}},1-p_{\mathrm{cal}}) \ge \tau$. Hence
$\mathbb{P}(\widehat Y = Y, A) \ge \tau\,\kappa$; divide by $\kappa>0$. ∎

### 13.3 Coverage–precision trade-off

**Proposition 13.4.** Under the hypotheses of Theorem 13.3, $\kappa(\tau)$ is
non-increasing in $\tau$ with $\kappa(\tfrac12)=1$, and
$\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{sel}}(\tau)$ is non-decreasing in $\tau$ on
$\{\tau : \kappa(\tau)>0\}$.

*Proof.* Monotonicity of $\kappa$ holds because
$A(\tau_2)\subseteq A(\tau_1)$ for $\tau_1<\tau_2$, and
$\kappa(\tfrac12)=1$ because the two cases of Definition 13.1 cover
$[0,1]$ at $\tau=\tfrac12$. Write $Z = \max(p_{\mathrm{cal}},1-p_{\mathrm{cal}})$.
On $A(\tau_2)$ we have $Z\ge\tau_2$ and on $A(\tau_1)\setminus A(\tau_2)$ we
have $Z<\tau_2$. Therefore, with
$\alpha = \mathbb{P}(A(\tau_2))/\mathbb{P}(A(\tau_1)) \in (0,1]$,

$$
\mathbb{E}[Z \mid A(\tau_1)]
= \alpha\,\mathbb{E}[Z\mid A(\tau_2)] + (1-\alpha)\,\mathbb{E}\big[Z \mid A(\tau_1)\setminus A(\tau_2)\big]
\;\le\; \mathbb{E}[Z\mid A(\tau_2)],
$$

since the second conditional expectation is $<\tau_2\le\mathbb{E}[Z\mid A(\tau_2)]$.
Theorem 13.3 identifies $\mathbb{E}[Z\mid A(\tau)]$ with
$\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{sel}}(\tau)$. ∎

### 13.4 Finite-sample and miscalibration correction

**Proposition 13.5 (degradation bound).** Drop perfect calibration and instead
assume the mean absolute calibration error on the accepted region satisfies

$$
\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lvert p_{\mathrm{cal}}(X) - \mathbb{P}(Y=1\mid X)\big\rvert \cdot \mathbf{1}_A\Big]
\;\le\; \varepsilon_{\mathrm{cal}} .
$$

Then

$$
\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{sel}} \;\ge\; \tau \;-\; \frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cal}}}{\kappa} .
$$

*Proof.* Let $p^{\ast}(X) = \mathbb{P}(Y=1\mid X)$ and
$\delta(X) = \lvert p_{\mathrm{cal}}(X)-p^{\ast}(X)\rvert$. On
$\{p_{\mathrm{cal}}\ge\tau\}$, correctness probability is
$p^{\ast} \ge p_{\mathrm{cal}} - \delta \ge \tau-\delta$; symmetrically on the
other branch. Hence
$\mathbb{P}(\widehat Y = Y, A) \ge \tau\kappa - \mathbb{E}[\delta\mathbf{1}_A]
\ge \tau\kappa - \varepsilon_{\mathrm{cal}}$. Divide by $\kappa$. ∎

**Remark 13.6.** Proposition 13.5 formalizes what the earlier draft stated
informally: an observed $\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{sel}} < \tau$ does not
contradict Theorem 13.3. It bounds the shortfall by
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cal}}/\kappa$ plus sampling noise, and shows the shortfall
is *amplified at low coverage* — precisely the regime where high $\tau$ is
attractive. Report $\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{sel}}$ with a binomial confidence
interval at the realized accepted count $\kappa n$.

## 14. Online co-adaptation

### 14.1 Replay buffer

**Definition 14.1 (causal rolling buffer).** At time $t$, with memory $M$,

$$
\mathcal{B}_t \;=\; \Big\{ \big(X_{a,s},\,Y_{a,s+1}\big) \;:\; a\in\mathcal{A},\ \ t-M \le s \le t-1 \Big\}.
$$

**Lemma 14.2.** Every element of $\mathcal{B}_t$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable.

*Proof.* $X_{a,s}$ is $\mathcal{F}_s$-measurable with $s\le t-1$, and
$Y_{a,s+1}$ is $\mathcal{F}_{s+1}$-measurable with $s+1\le t$. Both
$\sigma$-algebras are contained in $\mathcal{F}_t$. ∎

**Remark 14.3 (corrected index range).** The earlier draft defined the buffer
over $s\in[t-M+1,\,t]$, which admits the pair $(X_{a,t},Y_{a,t+1})$. Since
$Y_{a,t+1}$ is not $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable (Remark 1.2), that buffer
violates Definition 2.3 and would invalidate the causality claim of this
section. The upper limit must be $s = t-1$, as in Definition 14.1.

### 14.2 Update rule

After the close of day $t$, perform $K$ steps

$$
\theta \;\leftarrow\; \theta \;-\; \eta_{\mathrm{on}} \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}\big(\mathcal{B}_t\big),
\qquad \eta_{\mathrm{on}} \ll \eta_{\mathrm{off}} .
$$

### 14.3 Stability regularization

**Definition 14.4 (elastic weight consolidation).**

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{EWC}}(\theta)
= \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}(\theta)
+ \frac{\rho}{2}\sum_{k} F_k\big(\theta_k - \theta^{\mathrm{ref}}_k\big)^2,
$$

where $\theta^{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the offline-trained parameter and

$$
F_k = \mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{\partial \log p_\theta(Y\mid X)}{\partial \theta_k}\right)^{2}\right]_{\theta = \theta^{\mathrm{ref}}}
$$

is the diagonal Fisher information, estimated on
$\mathcal{T}^{\,\mathrm{emb}}_{\mathrm{tr}}$.

**Proposition 14.5 (online updates preserve causality).** If the model deployed
on day $t+1$ is the parameter $\theta^{(t)}$ produced by Definition 14.1 and
§14.2, then $\theta^{(t)}$ is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable and the forecast
$\widehat Y_{a,t+1}$ remains non-anticipative.

*Proof.* By induction on $t$. $\theta^{(T_2)}$ is
$\mathcal{F}_{T_2}$-measurable by Theorem 2.10. If $\theta^{(t-1)}$ is
$\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$-measurable, then $\theta^{(t)}$ is a Borel function of
$\theta^{(t-1)}$ and $\mathcal{B}_t$, the latter $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable by
Lemma 14.2; Lemma 2.4 applies. Combining with Lemma 6.3 and Corollary 7.3 gives
non-anticipativity. ∎

**Remark 14.6 (calibration under drift).** $g^{\star}$ is fitted once on the
validation block. If the encoder is updated online, the score distribution
drifts and $g^{\star}$ degrades; Proposition 13.5 then applies with a growing
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cal}}$. Either freeze $\theta$ between scheduled
recalibrations, or refit $g^{\star}$ on a trailing causal window
$\{s : t-M_g \le s \le t-1\}$, which is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable by the same
argument as Lemma 14.2.

## 15. Optimization

### 15.1 AdamW

With gradient $g_k$ at step $k$, decay rates $\beta_1,\beta_2\in(0,1)$,
learning rate $\eta$, and **decoupled weight decay $\gamma$** (distinct from the
loss weight $\lambda$ of §9.3):

$$
m_k = \beta_1 m_{k-1} + (1-\beta_1)g_k,
\qquad
v_k = \beta_2 v_{k-1} + (1-\beta_2)g_k^{\odot 2},
$$

$$
\widehat m_k = \frac{m_k}{1-\beta_1^{k}},
\qquad
\widehat v_k = \frac{v_k}{1-\beta_2^{k}},
$$

$$
\theta_{k+1} = \theta_k - \eta\,\frac{\widehat m_k}{\sqrt{\widehat v_k}+\epsilon} - \eta\,\gamma\,\theta_k .
$$

**Remark 15.1 (notation collision fixed).** The earlier draft used $\lambda$
for both the auxiliary loss weight and the AdamW decay. These are independent
hyperparameters and are now $\lambda$ and $\gamma$ respectively.

### 15.2 What can and cannot be guaranteed

**Remark 15.2 (non-convexity).** $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}$ is non-convex
in $\theta$ because of the recurrence in Definition 6.1. No global optimality
guarantee is available. Under standard smoothness and bounded-variance
conditions, first-order stochastic methods guarantee only approximate
stationarity,

$$
\min_{k \le K} \ \mathbb{E}\big\lVert \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}(\theta_k)\big\rVert \;\le\; \varepsilon,
$$

with $K = O(\varepsilon^{-4})$ in the general smooth non-convex setting. This
specification claims nothing stronger.

**Definition 15.3 (gradient clipping).** To control exploding gradients through
the recurrence, apply

$$
g_k \leftarrow g_k \cdot \min\left(1,\ \frac{c}{\lVert g_k\rVert_2}\right),
\qquad c > 0 .
$$

**Definition 15.4 (early stopping).** With patience $P$,

$$
k^{\star} = \arg\min_{k\le K} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(\theta_k),
$$

halting when no improvement occurs for $P$ consecutive epochs. The reported
model is $\theta_{k^{\star}}$.

**Remark 15.5 (selection is itself estimation).** $k^{\star}$ is chosen using
$\mathcal{T}^{\,\mathrm{emb}}_{\mathrm{va}}$ and is therefore
$\mathcal{F}_{T_2}$-measurable — admissible under Theorem 2.10. But
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}(\theta_{k^\star})$ is a *selected* minimum and is
downward-biased as an estimate of generalization. Only
$\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{te}}$ metrics may be reported as out-of-sample.

|---|---|---|

### 16.2 Mandatory baselines

A dual-head result is reportable only alongside:

1. **Constant predictor** — always predict the majority class of the training block.
2. **Single-head ablation** — identical encoder, $\lambda = 0$.
3. **Head-B ablation** — Head B present but with $\psi$ detached, isolating the
   co-adaptation effect of Theorem 10.2 from the mere presence of a second head.

**Remark 16.1.** Theorem 10.2 guarantees that the encoder is *displaced* by the
auxiliary task; baseline 3 is what determines whether that displacement is the
source of any measured gain.

| Variance view | Representation criterion | $\max\ \mathcal{E}(\psi)$ | Thm. 10.4, Prop. 11.2 |
| Isotonic calibration | Monotone probability correction | $p_{\mathrm{cal}}$ | Thm. 12.2 |
| Selective rule | Act or abstain | $\widehat Y \in \{0,1,\bot\}$ | Thm. 13.3 |
| Online update | Regime adaptation | $\theta^{(t)}$ | Prop. 14.5 |
| AdamW $+$ early stop | Optimization protocol | $\theta_{k^{\star}}$ | Rem. 15.2 |

The engine separates four mathematically distinct concerns:

1. **Causality** — every input is $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable and every split is chronological and embargoed (§2–§4).
2. **Representation** — one shared recurrent encoder produces a single latent state consumed by both heads (§5–§6).
3. **Estimation** — each head is a supervised map from that state to its own target, trained under one scalar objective (§7–§11).
4. **Decision** — calibration and thresholding convert a probability into an action or an abstention (§12–§13).
